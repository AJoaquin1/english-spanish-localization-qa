# English–Spanish Localization & QA Analysis

## Project Overview

This project demonstrates a professional **English-to-Spanish localization and quality assurance (QA) workflow** for customer-facing digital content.

The scenario simulates a U.S.-based digital commerce company preparing its platform for **Spanish-speaking customers in the United States and Latin America**. A dataset of 75 English source strings and their existing Spanish translations was reviewed for linguistic accuracy, consistency, naturalness, terminology, tone, and formatting.

The goal was not simply to translate the content, but to evaluate translation quality, identify systematic issues, establish localization standards, and provide a corrected, client-ready dataset supported by quantitative QA analysis.

---

## Business Objective

The objective was to improve the company's Spanish-language customer experience by:

- Evaluating existing English–Spanish translations
- Identifying linguistic and localization issues
- Standardizing terminology across the platform
- Maintaining a consistent professional **usted** register
- Adapting content to neutral Latin American Spanish
- Preserving variables, placeholders, numbers, and functional formatting
- Measuring translation quality through structured QA analysis

---

## Dataset

The project contains **75 customer-facing strings** across multiple areas of a digital commerce platform:

- Account
- Orders
- Checkout
- Shipping
- Returns & Refunds
- Subscriptions
- Customer Support
- Security & Privacy
- Notifications
- General UI content

Each record contains an English source string and an existing Spanish translation requiring professional QA review.

> **Note:** The dataset and company scenario were created specifically for this portfolio project to simulate a realistic localization QA assignment.

---

## Localization Standards

The target localization was defined as:

- **Language:** Spanish
- **Target Audience:** U.S. Hispanic and Latin American customers
- **Regional Approach:** Neutral Latin American Spanish
- **Register:** Professional `usted`

A terminology and style guide was established before completing the review to ensure consistent decisions across the dataset.

### Terminology Examples

| English | Preferred Spanish |
|---|---|
| Order | Pedido |
| Checkout | Finalizar compra |
| Shipping | Envío |
| Business days | Días hábiles |
| Tracking number | Número de seguimiento |
| Refund | Reembolso |
| Return | Devolución |
| Account | Cuenta |
| Subscription | Suscripción |
| Free trial | Prueba gratuita |

Functional placeholders such as `{first_name}`, `{order_number}`, `{date}`, and `{tracking_number}` were preserved during localization.

---

## QA Methodology

Each translation was evaluated across seven quality dimensions:

1. **Accuracy** — preservation of the original English meaning
2. **Grammar** — grammar, syntax, agreement, conjugation, and punctuation
3. **Terminology** — consistent use of standardized business terminology
4. **Tone / Register** — consistent professional `usted` voice
5. **Naturalness** — fluent Spanish rather than literal English constructions
6. **Completeness** — preservation of all relevant source information
7. **Formatting** — correct handling of placeholders, numbers, currency, and formatting

Issues were classified as **Minor**, **Major**, or **Critical** depending on their impact.

Translations requiring no changes were retained as **Approved**, providing a clear audit trail between the original and reviewed content.

## Results

### Overall QA Results

| Metric | Result |
|---|---:|
| Strings Reviewed | 75 |
| Approved Without Changes | 30 |
| Corrected | 45 |
| Initial Approval Rate | 40% |
| Correction Rate | 60% |
| Major Issues | 13 |
| Minor Issues | 32 |

No critical issues were identified.

### Key Findings

- **Naturalness** was the most frequent QA issue, with **23 corrections**.
- **Terminology** was the second most frequent issue, with **11 corrections**.
- The review identified **5 tone/register issues**, primarily involving inconsistent use of `tú` and `usted`.
- Customer Support content required corrections in **87.5%** of reviewed strings.
- Functional placeholders were preserved throughout the localization process.

## Example Correction

### English Source

> Your order is on its way!

### Original Spanish

> ¡Tu pedido está en camino!

### Reviewed Spanish

> ¡Su pedido está en camino!

**QA Classification:** Tone / Register — Major

**Reason:** The original translation used the informal `tú` register, while the established localization standard requires consistent professional `usted` language.
